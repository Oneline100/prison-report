Trapped and Suffering: How America's Prison System Causes Physical and Mental Suffering

Alexander Phouangmalay · Avaneesh Singh · Jayden Nguyen · Yash Shah
01 · Background

Introduction

America is known to have one of the greatest legal systems in the world. Some qualities of this justice system include its strict enforcement, affordability, and multiple chances, making it "orderly and fair." Despite these secure qualities, how it performs is essentially flawed because of what incarcerated people deal with inside the prison system.

02 · Overcrowding & Neglect

Overcrowding and Neglect

With over 2 million people in the US prison system at any given time, there have been many instances where prisoners are often crammed into small, claustrophobic cells, faced harsh heat and cold without proper ventilation and climate control, deal with unsanitary and disgusting food and sanitary conditions, and are deprived of access to educational, social, and legal services (Sawyer et al., 2026; Western, 2021). The conditions of overcrowding and ineffective services and facilities can lead to prisoners' loss of humanity and dignity, which can lead to an increase in mental illness, violence, assaults, and suicide (Western, 2021; Hopwood, 2021).

As one of the largest prison systems in the world that has billions invested, most inmates are often worse off than those who are homeless or living in poverty.

Overcrowding in prisons has been a persistent issue for almost 20 years, and continues to be a problem even today. As of 2019, Iowa leads the nation in prison overcrowding, with its prison population at 119% of its operational capacity — the number of prisoners a prison can accommodate (Spohn & Kiper, 2025). Over 10 states have reached or exceeded their operational capacity, with prison capacity exceeding design capacity, leaving insufficient physical space to house all prisoners (Spohn & Kiper, 2025).

With such overcrowding, this increases tension and stress in the prison community, leading to violent actions and deterioration (Spohn & Kiper, 2025; Western, 2021). One important example was in 2009, when most California prisons were overcrowded at 150% of their designed capacity; most prisoners were in disarray, with hundreds of cases of violence, extreme health risks, and as then-Governor Arnold Schwarzenegger reported, the suicide rate in 29 severely overcrowded prisons was approaching an average of one per week (Western, 2021).

Facility Conditions: Heat, Sanitation, and Services

With most prisons operating beyond their capacity, this has led to more issues with facilities and services provided to prisoners. Prisoners are often guaranteed certain privileges, such as their own beds, bathrooms, food, and educational, social, and legal services (Western, 2021). This is to ensure the prisoners can be reintegrated and rehabilitated into normal society after their release. However, with prisons at almost full or exceeding capacity, these services often break down and fail, leading to issues of unsanitary conditions, neglect, and non-compliance with prisoners.

One such issue is heat, which is more evident in southern states such as Alabama, Texas, and Georgia. Often, prison climate systems fail without warning, resulting in extremely sweltering conditions reaching 103–110°F for weeks on end (Kaufman et al., 2025). With understaffed facilities, poor conditions, and legal and political obstacles, systems at these prisons do not get repaired, leading to increased violence, heat stroke, dehydration, and even deaths — which accounted for almost 13% of all deaths in Texas prisons during summer months (Kaufman et al., 2025; Hopwood, 2021).

If heat and climate are not the issue, then lavatories, food, and prisoners' services are other points of failure. Often, correctional facilities have very unsanitary conditions in lavatories and food preparation areas, creating a risk of bacterial contamination and illness (Western, 2021; CT Office of Correction Ombuds, 2026). According to a report from the Connecticut Office of the Correction Ombuds, there is extensive damage to showers and bathrooms in most state prisons, and a lack of basic cleaning and hygiene supplies (2026). Additionally, there is a worrying amount of structural and facility damage, with mold, rust, and corrosion present at most facilities, raising concerns about injury and illness from mold, fiberglass, and rust (CT Office of Correction Ombuds, 2026).

Besides physical conditions, social, legal, and educational services are often nonexistent or in poor condition in prisons. Reports show that many services, such as libraries and phones, are often restricted or inoperable, with officials citing repairs or budget cuts (CT Office of Correction Ombuds, 2026). Without these services, people are unable to communicate with family and friends, furthering distress and isolation among prisoners. State legal assistance organizations that help poor and disadvantaged prisoners with legal work, parole, and other services are also critically important, yet these services are often understaffed and underfunded, leaving many prisoners turned away or neglected when seeking legal help (CT Office of Correction Ombuds, 2026).

“I wrote like 20 lawyers [regarding a medical grievance] but I never got a response back. I even wrote ILAP — they basically told me that I’m not going to get much out of the situation, so I just left it alone cause I could not find any help.” — Incarcerated Individual at the MacDougall–Walker Correctional Institution, CT Office of Correction Ombuds (2026)
Prisons are turning from a place of rehabilitation and redemption to more of a breeding ground for sickness, violence, and death.

With unsuitable, dangerous conditions in prisons, combined with mass incarceration and now immigration detentions, the consequences for prisoners continue to compound. The Department of Justice has already cut about $819.7 million in grants for federal rehabilitation and education resources, and in Florida, the governor has cut at least $840 million from the correctional budget for construction and repairs (Solomon & Pear, 2025; Joseph-Marc, 2025). Without any sort of reforms or policies to repair, oversee, and accommodate the growing prisoner population, the consequences will surely increase.

03 · Healthcare

Healthcare

Healthcare is designed to improve the “health and well-being of individuals and communities, including preventive, diagnostic, therapeutic, rehabilitative, maintenance, monitoring, and counseling services. ()” However, this is not the case for incarcerated individuals who lie in prison facilities. Many prisoners requiring medical attention were often ignored and left to die after being glanced at by the time it's too late. The staff’s ignorance or lack of care for the prisoners’ health leads to unnecessary suffering and can be seen as more inhumane than the death penalty itself. In order for prisons to become adequate at improving the health of prisoners are doctor-patient relationships, careful and more intensive attention towards prisoners, better regulations to ensure prisoners’ comfort, and the needs of those with conditions.

In order for prisons to adequately improve the health of prisoners, improvements must be made to doctor-patient relationships, care attention, regulations ensuring prisoners' comfort, and the needs of those with medical conditions. Illinois courts define the doctor-patient relationship as "a consensual relationship in which the patient knowingly seeks the physician's assistance and in which the physician knowingly accepts the person as a patient." This foundational trust is frequently absent in correctional settings.

Prison Work Conditions and Medical Neglect

Prison work conditions are especially harsh. Inmates have almost no labor rights and are paid very little — if they are paid at all — yet are required to perform grueling work. Critically, correctional facilities frequently fail to make adequate accommodations for individuals with special medical needs or physical disabilities. This lack of precaution places vulnerable populations at heightened risk of injury and deterioration.

Case Study · Healthcare Failure

Elliot Earl Williams: Death by Deliberate Indifference

The case of prisoner Elliot Earl Williams illustrates extreme medical neglect. Despite suffering a serious neurosurgical condition, Williams received inadequate diagnosis and treatment over a prolonged period. Scholars who examined his case characterized the institutional response as constituting "neglect, recklessness, and deliberate indifference in the face of a serious neurosurgical pathology" (Spence et al., 2025). His death highlighted how systemic failures in prison healthcare can transform a treatable condition into a fatal one.

Case Study · Youth Vulnerability

Children in Adult Prisons

Children placed in adult correctional facilities face compounded dangers. Research from the Equal Justice Initiative (2017) documents that youth in adult prisons are at dramatically elevated risk of physical and sexual abuse. In one documented instance from a juvenile detention facility, a young boy was subjected to sexual assault — an event that underscores the catastrophic failure of duty of care for the most vulnerable populations in the system. The EJI has long argued that all children are children, and the justice system must reflect that fundamental truth (Equal Justice Initiative, 2017).

Case Study · Disability Access

Wheelchair Accessibility and ADA Failures

Individuals with physical disabilities face particular dangers in facilities not equipped to accommodate them. Reports from juvenile and adult facilities document instances where individuals using wheelchairs were unable to access exits, bathrooms, or emergency routes — placing them in life-threatening situations during routine institutional operations. The failure to provide basic ADA-compliant accommodations violates both federal law and basic standards of human dignity.

The Marshall Project documented cases of prisoners dying of treatable conditions — including cancer — after being denied timely or adequate medical care (Marcus & Canning, 2025; Lartey, 2025). In many cases, inmates who flagged symptoms repeatedly were dismissed or undertreated until their conditions became terminal. These outcomes reflect a system where cost-cutting consistently overrides constitutional obligations to provide basic medical care.

04 · Violence & Safety

Violence and Safety

Within prisons, the security of incarcerated individuals is threatened by many threats, stemming from other inmates or staff. In the face of the criminal justice system, excessive force and inmate-on-inmate violence are highly prominent within prisons. There have been many violence-related cases that expose the correctional system’s failure to address the safety of inmates within prisons. Many prisons use a standardized system of “control” in order to keep prisoners in check. Even these universal methods pose both physical and psychological risks to the prisoners.

Alabama's prisons have been documented as the deadliest in the nation, with homicide rates far exceeding national averages for correctional facilities (Equal Justice Initiative, 2018). Witnessing violence — even without being a direct victim — causes lasting psychological trauma. A Prison Policy Initiative report (2020) found that exposure to violence in prison creates a form of trauma equivalent to combat exposure, with long-term consequences for mental health and behavior post-release.

Case Study · Staff Violence

Karl Taylor: Death After Guard Assault

Karl Taylor was an inmate who was first struck in the head by a prison guard. The abuse escalated to the point where he could not breathe during an altercation. He subsequently died. All three of the state correctional department, state police, and the local district attorney concluded that his death was caused by his declining health — despite the documented physical assault (Robbins, 2018). Taylor had schizophrenia and an additional disorder at the time, illustrating how mentally ill prisoners are especially vulnerable to staff violence while simultaneously being denied accountability for their deaths.

Within the standardized systems of prisons, recent statistics show that many inmates are often bound or chained to shackles in various methods, which often leads to obstructed blood flow if constantly held in this type of containment (Prison Policy Initiative, 2020). There has even been a specific case where this usage of confinement reflects the excessive force brought upon the correctional system by the standardized system of prisons. A prisoner with multiple illnesses was exposed to physical trauma and pepper-spray in the act of being shackled; they ultimately died after succumbing to physical injuries alongside restricted blood flow due to their sickle cell disease (Shapiro, 2025).

With increasingly sustained injuries and deaths every year, it suggests that the criminal justice system currently still fails to prioritize the safety of the prisoners. Even with the changing nature of allocation of its funds to different departments and uses each year, very few improvements have been made toward safety precautions for prisoners (Equal Justice Initiative 2018). This often contributes to the growing deaths or incident reports each year for prisoners, showing how small modern prison systems have grown in terms of physical welfare when compared to past cases and their history of incidents.

05 · Mental Health & Suicide

Mental Health and Suicide

The welfare of prisoners should remain one of the top priorities of the prison system and its guards, as their wellbeing depends heavily on the care they receive. The correctional system has consistently failed to provide adequate mental health support, often substituting temporary interventions for genuine treatment.

Case Study · Suicide Watch Failure

John Rudd: Dismissed and Abandoned

One such instance is seen with John Rudd. Despite making multiple suicidal claims and taking self-harming actions — including banging his head on the wall and attempting to snap his own neck — he was simply injected with an antipsychotic drug and was told that he "wasn't ill enough to require regular treatment" (Thompson et al., 2018). He was later removed from suicide watch, and his mental health was not being properly monitored. Instead of focusing on the underlying problem, the correctional system opted for temporary solutions — a pattern that proved tragically insufficient.

Case Study · Mental Illness and Guard Violence

Karl Taylor: Schizophrenia and Accountability

Another case is with Karl Taylor, an inmate who had schizophrenia and an additional mental health disorder. He was first struck in the head by a prison guard; the abuse escalated to the point where he lost the ability to breathe during an altercation. He later died. State authorities attributed his death to declining health rather than the assault — a conclusion that illustrates the profound lack of accountability in cases involving mentally ill inmates who are also victims of staff violence (Robbins, 2018).

As the chart above illustrates, over half of people in state prisons report mental health problems, but only 1 in 4 has received professional help since admission (Bureau of Justice Statistics, 2021). These cases and stark figures highlight the negligence and failure of the prison system to adequately monitor and care for its inmates' mental wellbeing.

Beyond individual cases, systemic data confirms the crisis. Nearly a fifth of state and federal prisons and a tenth of local jails had at least one suicide in 2019 (Watkins, 2021). Chronic untreated mental illness, isolation, loss of autonomy, and exposure to violence create conditions under which suicide becomes an all-too-common outcome.

Chronic Mental Health Needs Go Unmet

A 2022 Prison Policy Initiative report on chronic punishment found that the mental and physical health needs of people in state prisons are drastically unmet (Wang, 2022). People enter prison with higher rates of mental illness than the general population, yet correctional healthcare systems are consistently underfunded and understaffed. Rather than providing treatment, the prison environment frequently worsens pre-existing conditions and generates new ones through trauma, isolation, and neglect.

06 · Rehabilitation

Rehabilitation

Over the past few years, there have been multiple scenarios where prison conditions were the reason behind transforming the outlook of incarcerated individuals for the worse. There were even cases where innocent victims who were wrongfully convicted were influenced after release to commit a crime due to mental pressures. In fact, recent reports over the years show high risks of mortality, elevated depression levels, worsened mood levels, and disorders being more prominent among formerly incarcerated individuals (Columbia Justice Lab, 2024).

In new cases, empirical evidence suggests that even court-ordered releases that deem the convicted person "stable" still exhibit criminogenic tendencies post-release (Lofstrom, 2024). These consistent patterns highlight the critical error within the prison system's approach to effectively rehabilitating incarcerated individuals.

Furthermore, even when individuals are deemed "stable" upon their release, this doesn't necessarily mean they are fully rehabilitated. Without consistent support and structured rehabilitation programs both during and after incarceration, many individuals leave prison without ever confronting the underlying factors that influenced their behavior. Within the past 10 years, roughly 79% of incarcerated U.S. individuals were more likely to be repeat offenders after previous incarceration (Alper, 2018).

These consistent gaps in rehabilitation suggest that the likelihood of recidivism will keep increasing throughout the years. With individuals not being provided with effective, long-term rehabilitation programs, they often leave prison without the tools needed to successfully reintegrate into society. This worsens their future mental health and financial situation, as it becomes increasingly harder to secure daily necessities such as finding jobs, applying for insurance or loans, and having affordable housing (Nilsson et al., 2023). This ultimately contributes to a common trend of higher death rates, recidivism, or homelessness.

In tandem with these ongoing issues, these drawbacks in the prison system reflect the broader flaws of the justice system. Rather than prioritizing long-term reform, the justice system tends to focus on punishment and containment, which leaves little room for support or funding of a proper rehabilitation system (Nilsson et al., 2023). Even with growing awareness of these issues, many correctional facilities still lack the proper funding and resources to implement effective rehabilitation. As a result, the justice system continues to fall short of its intended purpose, forcing it to maintain patterns of recidivism and a systematic failure to implement successful reintegration into society.
