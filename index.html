Trapped and Suffering:
How America's Prison System Causes Physical and Mental Suffering

An investigation into the failures of rehabilitation and containment in modern American corrections.

Alexander Phouangmalay  ·  Avaneesh Singh  ·  Jayden Nguyen  ·  Yash Shah
01  ·  Introduction / Background

A Flawed Performance

America is known to have one of the greatest legal systems in the world. Some qualities of this justice system include its strict enforcement, affordability, and multiple chances, making it "orderly and fair." Despite these secure qualities, how it performs is essentially flawed because of what incarcerated people deal with inside the prison system.

02  ·  Overcrowding & Neglect

Substandard Living Conditions

With over 2 million people in the US prison system at any given time, there have been many instances where prisoners are often crammed into small, claustrophobic cells, faced harsh heat and cold without proper ventilation and climate control, deal with unsanitary and disgusting food and sanitary conditions, and are deprived of access to educational, social, and legal services (Sawyer et al., 2026; Western, 2021). The conditions of overcrowding and ineffective services and facilities can lead to prisoners' loss of humanity and dignity, which can lead to an increase in mental illness, violence, assaults, and suicide (Western, 2021; Hopwood, 2021).

As one of the largest prison systems in the world that has billions invested, most inmates are often worse off than those who are homeless or living in poverty. Overcrowding in prisons has been a persistent issue for almost 20 years now, and continues to be a problem in prisons even today. As of 2019, Iowa leads the nation in prison overcrowding, with its prison population at 119% of its operational capacity (Spohn & Kiper, 2025). Over 10 states have reached or exceeded their operational capacity, with prison capacity exceeding design capacity, leaving insufficient physical space to house all prisoners (Spohn & Kiper, 2025). With such overcrowding and crowded spaces, this increases tension and stress in the prison community, leading to violent actions and deterioration (Spohn & Kiper, 2025; Western, 2021).

03  ·  Healthcare Neglect

Doctor-Patient Relationships & Welfare Breakdown

Healthcare is designed to improve the "health and well-being of individuals and communities." However, this is not the case for incarcerated individuals. The staff's ignorance or lack of care for the prisoners' health leads to unnecessary suffering and can be seen as more inhumane than the death penalty itself.

04  ·  Violence & Safety

Welfare Under Threat

Within prisons, the welfare of incarcerated individuals is routinely placed at risk by institutional violence and staff misconduct. Overcrowded facilities create environments where tension escalates quickly. Reports from the Prison Policy Initiative (2020) document the lasting psychological harm that comes from witnessing violence in prison.

05  ·  Mental Health & Suicide

A Crisis of Care

The welfare of the prisoners should remain a top priority. In the case of John Rudd, despite multiple suicidal claims, he was removed from suicide watch and his mental health was not monitored properly (Thompson et al., 2018). Instead of focusing on the underlying problem, the correctional system chose to resolve the situation with only temporary solutions.

06  ·  Rehabilitation Failure

Consistent Pattern Errors

Recent reports show high risks of mortality, elevated depression levels, and worsened mood levels post-release. Within the past 10 years, roughly 79% of incarcerated U.S. individuals were more likely to be repeat offenders after previous incarceration (Alper, 2018).
