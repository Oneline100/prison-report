01 · Background

Introduction

America is known to have one of the greatest legal systems in the world[cite: 4]. Some qualities of this justice system include its strict enforcement, affordability, and multiple chances, making it "orderly and fair"[cite: 5]. Despite these secure qualities, how it performs is essentially flawed because of what incarcerated people deal with inside the prison system[cite: 6].

02 · Overcrowding

Overcrowding and Neglect [cite: 7]

With over 2 million people in the US prison system at any given time, there have been many instances where prisoners are often crammed into small, claustrophobic cells, faced harsh heat and cold without proper ventilation and climate control, deal with unsanitary and disgusting food and sanitary conditions, and are deprived of access to educational, social, and legal services (Sawyer et al., 2026; Western, 2021)[cite: 8]. The conditions of overcrowding and ineffective services and facilities can lead to prisoners' loss of humanity and dignity, which can lead to an increase in mental illness, violence, assaults, and suicide (Western, 2021; Hopwood, 2021)[cite: 9].

03 · Healthcare

Healthcare [cite: 117]

Healthcare is designed to improve the "health and well-being of individuals and communities, including preventive, diagnostic, therapeutic, rehabilitative, maintenance, monitoring, and counseling services"[cite: 118, 119]. However, this is not the case for incarcerated individuals who lie in prison facilities[cite: 119]. Many prisoners requiring medical attention were often ignored and left to die after being glanced at by the time it's too late[cite: 119]. The staff's ignorance or lack of care for the prisoners' health leads to unnecessary suffering and can be seen as more inhumane than the death penalty itself[cite: 119].

05 · Mental Health

Mental Health/Suicide [cite: 130]

The welfare of the prisoners should remain as one of the top priorities of the prison system and the guards, as their wellbeing depends heavily on the caretaking from them[cite: 131]. One such instance is seen with John Rudd. Despite making multiple suicidal claims and actions... he was simply injected with an antipsychotic drug and was told that he "wasn't ill enough of require regular treatment" (Thompson, et al. 2018)[cite: 132]. Furthermore, he was later removed from the suicide watch and his mental health wasn't being monitored properly[cite: 133].

06 · Rehabilitation

Rehabilitation [cite: 162]

Over the past few years, there have been multiple scenarios where prison conditions were the reason behind transforming the outlook of incarcerated victims[cite: 163]. In fact, recent reports over the years show high risks of mortality, elevated depression levels, worsened mood levels, and disorders being more prominent (Columbia Justice Lab, 2024)[cite: 165]. Within the past 10 years, roughly 79% of incarcerated U.S. individuals were more likely to be repeat offenders after previous incarceration (Alper, 2018)[cite: 170].
